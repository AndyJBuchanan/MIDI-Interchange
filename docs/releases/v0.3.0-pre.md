**MIDI Interchange 0.3.0 (pre-release)**: a MIDI palette for moving MIDI between music apps. Collect clips, shape them with trims and splits, choose tracks and ranges, then drag or export the result to another app.

This release keeps your workspace between runs, adds clip splitting and track export-channel overrides, and makes tempo maps and markers easier to work with. It has no playback, instruments or note editing, and never changes your source files.

## What's new since 0.2.2

**Return to your workspace.**

- The app restores imported MIDI, clips and trims, track names and colours, mute/solo, channel overrides, ranges and their track locks, tempo, export settings and your view. Undo history starts fresh.
- MIDI is kept in the session itself, so restoring doesn't depend on the original files still being available.
- Sessions save on exit and in the background after two minutes when you've been inactive for at least 15 seconds. Uninterrupted editing defers the background save.
- Only one app instance runs. Opening files from Finder or Explorer imports them into the existing window; launching without files brings it forward.
- Damaged or incompatible sessions are handled with a notice and a usable empty workspace. Session compatibility has its own schema version.

**Split clips without losing their original MIDI.**

- Hold **Cmd on Mac / Ctrl on Windows** over a clip: scissors and a vertical preview show the split position. Click to split; hold Shift to bypass snap.
- The two pieces stay adjacent, and the whole split is one undo step. Both halves can be moved, copied or trimmed independently.
- Either half can be extended back into the original MIDI; **Restore Full Clip** recovers its original full extent. A note crossing the split is released and restarted there on export.
- Move and copy have distinct cursors, including a move arrow with a plus for copying. Copying takes precedence over trimming at clip edges.
- **Shift during move/copy:** bypass snap on the source track; preserve the original time on another or new track. Returning to the source track resumes unsnapped movement.

**Choose a track's export MIDI channel.**

- Click its colour box for the palette, colour picker and **Export MIDI channel** menu.
- Keep **Original channels**, or override with **1–16**. The number appears in the colour box.
- The override maps every channel message from every clip on that track in saves and drag-out, without rewriting source MIDI. Undo restores the previous choice.
- Track and global M/S buttons sit together, with a matching-sized colour box on track headers.

**Find tempo maps and read markers more easily.**

- Right-click the ruler, tempo row or marker row for **Tempo Map**. A track's menu also offers **Use This Track's Tempo Map** when available.
- Markers have their own row and use their track's colour. The row follows global mute/solo, so it shows the markers from the globally enabled tracks.
- The **Markers** toolbar button (also Timing ▸ **Show Markers**) hides the row without removing markers from exports. Its setting is remembered.

## Known limitations

- **Reason automation lane:** importing channel-overridden MIDI can add an unwanted Controller #254 lane. The override works; delete the lane in Reason.
- Cursor switching occasionally stops working. This predates clip splitting; its cause is unknown and investigation is deferred.
- On macOS, Esc cancellation of incoming Finder/Cubase drags remains unresolved. Moving the pointer outside the window restores the timeline view.
- Drag-and-drop support depends on the other app. Cubase drags one clip at a time; Logic can't drag MIDI out. Reason can split a mixed-channel track into separate tracks; use an export-channel override to unify it. Some apps don't adopt tempo from a drag; open the saved MIDI file instead.
- The marker header follows global mute/solo, so a locked range can export markers that aren't shown there.
- There is no project Save/Open interface; session persistence keeps the current workspace automatically.

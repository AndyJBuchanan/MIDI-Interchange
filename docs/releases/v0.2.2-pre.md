**MIDI Interchange 0.2.2 (pre-release)**: a MIDI palette for moving MIDI between apps. Drag in or import MIDI from one or more apps, pick the tracks and ranges you want, and drag them out or export to another.

This release gives you finer control over what you hand on: trim clips, choose tracks with mute and solo, and lock a range's own track choices. Dragging in can now reach anywhere in the song.

It isn't a DAW or an arranger: there's no playback, no instruments and no note editing. This is still a pre-release, so please report anything odd.

## What's new since 0.2.0

**Trim clips.**

- To trim a clip, **Option-drag** (Alt-drag on Windows) its left or right edge. Notes outside the trim are left out of exports.
- The edge snaps to the grid like a move does; hold Shift to place it freely. It can't go past where the clip's MIDI originally started or ended.
- To put both edges back, right-click the clip ▸ **Restore Full Clip**.
- A note that started before a trimmed start sounds from the edge. A note that runs past a trimmed end is released at it.

**Mute and solo replace the include tickbox.**

- Each track has **M** (mute) and **S** (solo). Several tracks can be soloed at once.
- Option-click **S** for an exclusive solo. Option-click **M** to set every track's mute at once.
- The boxed **M** and **S** buttons above the tracks clear all mutes or all solos.
- With no ranges, mute and solo decide which tracks go into an export.
- **Save This Track…** still saves that track, even if it's muted.

**Ranges follow mute and solo, unless you lock them.**

- A new range follows the global mute and solo; its greyed checkboxes show what it will export.
- Click the **padlock** in a range's header to lock it. Its checkboxes become live, so you can choose that range's tracks yourself. A locked range ignores later mute and solo changes.
- Unlock it, and it follows the global result again straight away.
- Right-click a range for **Include All Tracks** and **Exclude All Tracks**, which both lock it first, and **Unlock**. These replace **Solo for Export** and **Reset to Track Toggles**.

**Dragging in reaches the whole song.**

- Hold an incoming drag near either edge of the timeline to scroll it. The timeline grows as you go right, so you can drop anywhere later in the song.
- A marker on the ruler shows the drop time, even when the destination track is scrolled out of view.
- Dropping keeps only the length needed for what you dropped. Leaving the window mid-drag, or cancelling the "Place dropped files" question, puts the view back where it was.

**Export names.** An unnamed range is named `Range N` in file names, as on screen. Saving several separate files suggests `Export`, giving `Export - Verse.mid`, `Export - Range 2.mid` and so on.

**Smaller things:**

- The app now has its own icon on macOS and Windows.
- There's an **About** button at the right end of the toolbar.
- The README usage guide is rewritten, with annotated screenshots.

## Known limitations

- **Drag and drop with other apps depends on what each app supports.**
  - On macOS it has been tried with Cubase 16, Reason 14, Staccato.ai, Scaler 3, RapidComposer 6 and Logic Pro. It works as far as each app allows.
  - Cubase drags out one clip at a time, and Logic can't drag MIDI out.
  - Reason splits a track that mixes MIDI channels into one track per channel.
  - Dragging into Cubase and Reason doesn't bring the tempo along; opening the saved file does.
  - Windows drag and drop hasn't been checked with DAWs yet.
- On macOS, Esc doesn't cancel a drag coming in from Finder or Cubase. That depends on the other app. Moving the pointer out of the window restores the view.
- On Windows, opening several files at once from Explorer opens several windows. Drag them onto one window instead.
- Workspaces can't be saved or reopened yet; keeping your session between runs is planned.
